# /prep: one company, one round, one page

Read `CLAUDE.md` first.

**The answers are already in the person's experience. This organizes and fact-checks. It never
writes their voice and never invents a reason they hold.**

## 0. Intake

- Which company, which round, who's on the call, and when. If they don't know who, prep the round
  generally and say so.
- Find the application folder. If `notes.md` is there, the fit read and the gaps are already done.
- No folder means there was no /apply. Pull the posting first, or the sheet is guesswork.

## 1. Check before anything else

- Is the posting still up? A call for a closed role changes the conversation.
- Has anything in `CLAIMS.md` changed since the fit read?

## 2. Research the company

Their product, who they serve, recent news, and the team the role sits on. **Has the person used the
product?** If not, say so at the top of the sheet and tell them to try it before the call.

## 3. Write `<folder>/prep.md`

One page. If it doesn't fit on one screen, it won't get read before a call.

1. **The call:** company, role, round, who, when.
2. **Their three biggest asks**, in their words, from `notes.md`.
3. **The evidence for each**, one line apiece, from `CLAIMS.md`.
4. **The gaps they'll probe**, from the fit read, each with an honest answer. If there's no answer
   yet, write the question with "needs your answer" beside it. Never invent one.
5. **Three questions to ask them**, ones that could only come from having looked.

## 4. Hand it over

Say the three asks, which gaps have answers, which still need one, and the one thing to do before the
call. Then stop.

**Per round, not per company.** A recruiter screens for fit, a manager for craft and judgment, a
director for scope. Rebuild the sheet for each round.

## 5. After the interview

Add to `prep.md`: the date, who was there, every question they asked, and what the person wished
they'd said. A new fact with proof goes into `CLAIMS.md`.

## Guardrails

- Never write "why this company" for them. Ask one question instead.
- Never close a gap with a claim. A gap gets an honest answer.
- It's notes, not a script. Reading generated sentences aloud is audible.
